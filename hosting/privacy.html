Two different products, honestly described

Gmail Agent runs on your computer. Without the included AI, it has no server: it talks to Google directly with your own credentials, stores everything locally, and sends nothing to us. With the included AI turned on, that changes in one specific way, and this policy describes exactly how. We do not claim that mail never leaves your computer when you have chosen an option under which it does.

What stays on your computer, always

  • Your Google refresh and access tokens, held in your operating system’s credential store.
  • Every Gmail message body, attachment, inline image, and raw HTML.
  • The local database of what the tool did: runs, actions, rules, and cached message metadata.
  • Your local diagnostic logs, which are written without message content or credentials.

We never receive your Google credentials. Gmail API traffic goes from your computer to Google and is not proxied through us.

What is sent to us, with the included AI on

During a preview or a cleanup, each unresolved message may be classified automatically — not only messages you select individually. For each such message we receive:

  • the sender display name and address;
  • the subject and the date the message was sent;
  • your timezone, so relative dates in the message resolve correctly;
  • a derived yes/no bulk-mail signal (not the underlying headers);
  • the names of your custom Gmail labels, so the model reuses one instead of inventing a near-duplicate;
  • bounded plain text from the message body, or Gmail’s short preview snippet.

When you ask for a draft, we receive:

  • for a reply: the sender, subject, and bounded plain text of the message you are replying to, plus any guidance you typed;
  • for a new message: the recipient, subject, and the purpose you typed.

This launch version does not sample your Sent mail to learn your writing style. If that feature returns, it will have its own separate disclosure and its own explicit opt-in; it will not ride along on this one.

What is never sent

  • Attachments, inline images, raw HTML, and remote content.
  • Google OAuth tokens of any kind.
  • Gmail data unrelated to the specific operation you triggered.
  • Your local database, action history, or rules.
  • Browser cookies or storage.

What we store

We store two things, both keyed to a pseudonymous identifier:

  • A consent receipt: the version of this disclosure you accepted, when you accepted it, and whether it is active or revoked.
  • Usage counters: request counts per minute, day, and month, and token totals, so fair-use limits can be enforced and costs monitored.

The identifier is a keyed hash of the stable account identifier Google returns at sign-in. We do not store your email address — the app does not even request the scope that would give us one — and we cannot reverse the identifier without a key held in Google Secret Manager.

We do not store message content. Message text passes through memory to produce the classification or draft you asked for and is not written to a database, a log, an error report, or a trace. Our request logs record the operation, the pseudonymous identifier, a status, a duration, and a token count.

  • [Model service] — processes the message text described above to produce the classification or draft.

We do not route your content to a provider or route that does not meet that requirement; if no compliant route is available, the request fails and Gmail Agent falls back to rules only rather than quietly using a weaker one.

Limited Use

Gmail Agent’s use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Google user data is used only to provide the features described in the app and this policy, is not transferred except as described here, is not used for advertising, and is not used to train or improve generalized models.

Your choices

  • Run with no AI at all, or with your own AI provider key, and nothing reaches us.
  • Turn the included AI off at any time with gmail setup.
  • Disconnect this computer entirely; that revokes the session and erases local credentials.
  • Revoke Google access at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Request deletion of your consent receipt and counters — see Data deletion.

Changes

If this policy changes in substance, the disclosure version changes with it. The app will stop sending message text and ask you to read and accept the new version before continuing. An old acceptance is never carried forward across a substantive change.
